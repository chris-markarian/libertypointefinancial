Indexed universal life is permanent life insurance with a death benefit and a cash value that can earn interest credits linked to a market index, within limits. It has ongoing costs, requires underwriting, and needs to be funded well to stay in force. It can suit some people and not others, and term life, whole life and other options are often worth comparing first.

Start with why you need life insurance

The right policy starts with the need, not the product. A licensed professional can help you think through:

Who depends on you

Income your family would need to replace, and for how long.

What you owe

Mortgage, other debts, and final expenses.

How long the need lasts

Many needs end when children are grown or a mortgage is paid off. Term life is often the lower-cost way to cover a need that ends.

How IUL works

IUL is permanent life insurance. It’s life insurance first, not an investment in the stock market or an index.

Death-benefit protection

The policy pays a death benefit to your beneficiaries, generally free of federal income tax. It stays in force only while the policy has enough value to cover its charges.

Premiums and charges

Each premium pays for the cost of insurance and policy charges first. Whatever remains goes to the policy’s cash value. The cost of insurance generally rises as you age.

Potential cash-value features

Cash value may earn interest credits linked to an index, subject to a cap, a participation rate and a floor. You may be able to access cash value through loans or withdrawals, which reduce the death benefit and cash value and can have tax consequences.

Costs, limits and risks to understand

Limits on growth

  • Caps and participation rates limit how much index growth is credited
  • Insurers can change caps and participation rates over time
  • Index credits can be zero in some years

Costs

  • Cost of insurance and policy charges every month
  • Surrender charges if you cancel in the early years, often 10 to 15 years
  • Riders add cost

Underwriting

  • Health and financial underwriting apply
  • Approval and price aren’t guaranteed

Lapse and loan risk

  • Underfunding or charges that outpace credits can cause a lapse
  • Policy loans accrue interest and reduce benefits
  • A lapse with a loan outstanding can create taxable income
No guarantees of returns. Any illustration you see in a consultation is a hypothetical based on assumptions that can change. Past index performance doesn’t predict future credits.

Alternatives worth comparing

A consultation should cover these, and the right answer may be one of them, or a mix.

Term life

Coverage for a set period at a lower starting cost. No cash value. Often fits needs that end.

Whole life

Permanent coverage with guaranteed premiums and cash value, usually at a higher premium.

Guaranteed universal life

Permanent death-benefit coverage with little focus on cash value, often at a lower premium than whole life.

Saving or investing separately

For example, retirement accounts, alongside term coverage. A financial or tax professional can help compare.

Common questions

Is there a cost?

No. The consultation is free and there is no obligation to buy anything.

Will I be sold a policy?

The consultation is educational. Dave may discuss options, including ones that aren’t IUL, and you decide whether to do anything.

Is IUL an investment?

No. IUL is life insurance. Its cash value isn’t invested in the stock market or an index. Interest credits are calculated using an index, within the policy’s caps and participation rates.

How do policy loans work?

You may be able to borrow against cash value. Loans accrue interest and reduce the death benefit and cash value. If the policy lapses with a loan outstanding, it can create taxable income.

Did the 2021 tax change make IUL better?

The 2021 update to Section 7702 of the tax code changed the interest-rate assumptions used to test life insurance contracts, which affects how new policies can be designed. It doesn’t make IUL the right choice for any particular person. A tax professional can explain how it applies to you.
